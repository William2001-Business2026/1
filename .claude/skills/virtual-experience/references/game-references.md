# Reference games: GTA VI (main), Red Dead Redemption 2 and Ghost of Tsushima

**GTA VI is the main benchmark**; RDR2 and Ghost of Tsushima support it. Use them as the yardstick when planning a feature and when judging screenshots: "would this hold up next to GTA VI?" first, then RDR2 and Ghost of Tsushima.

Judge GTA VI only by what Rockstar has shown publicly (trailers, screenshots, the released game where available), and say so.

Take their *qualities*, never their *content*. Do not reproduce their names, characters, story, maps, art, music, UI or assets, even renamed. Everything built is original and fits the experience's own setting (for Hale Moku, Hawaiʻi).

## GTA VI: a realistic world that holds up close (main reference)

| Quality | What it means for a build |
|---|---|
| Water that behaves | A sea with real swell, breaking surf, foam and wet sand; refraction and depth colour; reflections of sky and shore; wakes and splashes; rivers that bend around rocks and blend into the sea at their mouth. |
| Light as the look | A real sun and moon on a 24-hour clock; long soft shadows at sunrise and sunset, short at noon; light that bounces (ambient occlusion and probes in a browser, since ray tracing is not available); eye adaptation; haze and light shafts; wet surfaces that shine. |
| Dense, varied vegetation | The right species, many of them, close together, with ground clutter; no popping between levels of detail; lit and shadowed like the ground they stand on. |
| Wildlife with lives | Species-correct animals with routines (feeding, drinking, resting), reactions to the player and to each other, feet that meet the ground, and their own sounds. |
| People with detail | A realistic body, skin and clothes, feet placed on slopes, hands on what they hold, natural turns and transitions. |
| Scale with density | A large streamed world where the area you are in is dense and the far land is still there, at real heights. |
| Physical traces | Footprints and wet prints, things that fall and settle, structures that stand only if supported. |

## Red Dead Redemption 2: a living, physical world

| Quality | What it means for a build |
|---|---|
| A world that lives without you | Animals have routines (graze, drink, flee, hunt) and react to you, weather and time. Nothing waits frozen for the player. |
| Physical, grounded detail | Things have weight: footsteps match the surface, wet ground darkens and shines, mud and puddles after rain, items fall and roll with physics. |
| Hunting and stealth | Approach slowly and quietly (Shift to sneak); animals notice you at a distance by species; a clean strike matters; butchering is a step; meat is cooked to be useful. |
| Natural light and atmosphere | Sun and sky drive everything; haze and atmospheric perspective at distance; golden hour is warm and long; nights are dark but readable; fog settles in hollows and forests. |
| Ecosystems by habitat | Each species lives where it would: forest, grassland edge, mountain, shore, sky, water. |
| Sound as half the world | Layered ambience per place (wind with height, water near rivers, birds by day, insects and owls by night), positional sources, and rain that sounds like what it falls on. |
| Restrained interface | A small label and one-line hints, with no clutter; the world itself tells you (journal entries written after the fact). |

## Ghost of Tsushima: beauty, wind and composition

| Quality | What it means for a build |
|---|---|
| Wind as the signature | One strong, visible wind moves all grass, leaves, fronds and cloth together, with travelling gusts; fields of grass ripple in waves and part around the player. |
| Dense, painterly vegetation | Fields are full, not sparse; colour blocks (golden grass, red blossoms, green canopies) read clearly from afar; leaves and flowers drift in the air. |
| Composition and colour grading | Every zone has its own palette and mood; strong sky gradients, bloom on bright skies, a filmic grade; views framed so landmarks (mountain, waterfall, great trees) pull you toward them. |
| Exploration that rewards | Hidden places (a spring behind boulders, a summit, a waterfall pool) with a small reward on discovery: a name appearing, a journal page. |
| Elevation and vistas | Climbing gives a payoff view; clouds and mist sit on peaks; wind is stronger and louder up high. |
| Photo-worthy moments | Light shafts, spray and mist at waterfalls, petals and fireflies, a sunset over the sea; photo mode (P) and a clean, hideable interface. |

## How to use them while working

- **Planning:** for each new feature, name which qualities above it should deliver (for example, "forest: ecosystems by habitat, dense vegetation, wind, layered sound").
- **Judging:** compare screenshots to the qualities, not to the games' actual frames. Typical gaps: too sparse, too smooth, too evenly lit, too clean, too quiet, a hard edge where nature is soft.
- **Scope honesty:** these are AAA games built by large studios. Say plainly where a browser build falls short (animation depth, asset detail, view distance), and pick the improvements that close the biggest gap per hour of work.
